# Measurement Items (Core Constructs)

- **36 core items (Q6–Q41)**, rated on a **5-point Likert scale** (1 = Strongly disagree … 5 = Strongly agree).
- Items marked **(R)** were **reverse-coded** before analysis.
- Each construct score = the **mean of its four items**.

Item-to-construct mapping:

| Construct | Items |
|---|---|
| Neuro-Trust (NT) | Q10, Q11, Q15, Q16 |
| System Trust (ST) | Q12, Q14, Q20, Q22 |
| Perceived Control (PC) | Q13, Q19, Q23, Q27 |
| Feedback Stability (FS) | Q9, Q29, Q35, Q40 |
| Perceived Feedback Explainability (PFE) | Q17, Q18, Q21, Q36 |
| Wearing & Calibration Experience (WCE) | Q6, Q7, Q8, Q34 |
| Privacy & Surveillance Concern (PSC) | Q24, Q25, Q26, Q28 |
| Perceived Attention Regulation Value (PARV) | Q30, Q31, Q32, Q33 |
| Reuse Intention (RI) | Q37, Q38, Q39, Q41 |

## Neuro-Trust (NT)
- **Q10.** When I feel the device can capture my state during training, I find the attention feedback it provides credible.
- **Q11.** When the device's feedback disagrees with my subjective feelings, I still consider that it may reflect changes in my attention state that I did not notice myself.
- **Q15.** When I cannot clearly judge whether I am focused, I believe the device's feedback offers a credible reference for my attention state.
- **Q16 (R).** Even after this training, I would still doubt whether the device's feedback truly reflects my actual attention state.

## System Trust (ST)
- **Q12 (R).** I would doubt whether the system's attention judgments have a reliable basis rather than being produced arbitrarily.
- **Q14.** If the training report is largely consistent with my actual feelings, I consider the system's judgments reliable.
- **Q20 (R).** Even though the smart headband appears technologically advanced, I would worry that its actual judgment ability is not as reliable as it looks.
- **Q22.** Based on my hands-on experience just now, I consider this smart-headband attention-training system generally trustworthy.

## Perceived Control (PC)
- **Q13.** I can change the score or feedback during training by actively adjusting my state of focus.
- **Q19 (R).** When I have tried adjusting my focus but the feedback does not change accordingly, I feel I can hardly control the training process.
- **Q23.** After this training, I roughly know how to adjust my focus to change the feedback.
- **Q27.** Overall, I feel able to control the feedback changes in this training to some extent.

## Feedback Stability (FS)
- **Q29.** During this training, I felt the changes in the system's feedback were relatively coherent.
- **Q9 (R).** If I feel my focus has not changed noticeably but the system feedback changes greatly, I would find the feedback unstable.
- **Q35.** When the system feedback changes, I feel the changes are usually presented gradually and smoothly.
- **Q40.** During this training, I could perceive that the feedback changes followed a certain pattern overall.

## Perceived Feedback Explainability (PFE)
- **Q21.** The attention score and level in the training report let me clearly know what level my attention state was at.
- **Q36.** The text, charts, and prompts in the training report are presented clearly and I can understand the information relatively easily.
- **Q18.** The adjustment suggestions in the training report let me know how I should adjust my attention next.
- **Q17.** The report's explanation of how the score was produced helped me understand my performance in this training.

## Wearing & Calibration Experience (WCE)
- **Q6 (R).** The slight tightness or foreign-body sensation when wearing the smart headband would interfere with my focus.
- **Q7.** When the device connection and calibration go smoothly, I consider the wearing and calibration experience good.
- **Q34 (R).** If obvious tightness, foreign-body sensation, or pressure appears during extended wear, I would find this way of wearing unacceptable.
- **Q8 (R).** If the connection or calibration requires long waiting or repeated operations, I would find the whole preparation process cumbersome.

## Privacy & Surveillance Concern (PSC)
- **Q24.** I would worry that my attention data might be viewed by others without my consent.
- **Q28.** If my attention data were used to evaluate my learning attitude or performance, I would feel uncomfortable.
- **Q26.** If teachers or parents could see my attention data, I would clearly feel monitored.
- **Q25.** If my attention data cannot be kept visible only to me and stored anonymously, I would worry that my privacy is not adequately protected.

## Perceived Attention Regulation Value (PARV)
- **Q31.** During training, I could feel the game feedback helping me refocus my attention.
- **Q32.** I feel this training helped me become more clearly aware of changes in my attention state.
- **Q33.** After this experience, I regard helping me regulate my attention state as an important value of such products.
- **Q30.** I feel the smart-headband attention training is helpful for improving my focus.

## Reuse Intention (RI)
- **Q37.** If there were another similar smart-headband attention-training opportunity, I would be willing to take part again.
- **Q38.** If I had attention-training needs in the future, I would be willing to choose the smart-headband training method again.
- **Q39.** I would be willing to keep using such systems in my future study or daily focus practice.
- **Q41.** If I needed to continue attention training later, I would be willing to use such systems repeatedly at a certain frequency.
